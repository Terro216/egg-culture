# Continuous rhythm growth and usable downhill speed — 2026-09-24

The [dynamic rhythm update](egg-road-dynamic.md) supersedes the EGG3 boost curve, stroke thresholds and charge decay below. The speed-course contact and drive changes remain in use.

The previous speed bonus depended only on a charge clamped to 1. A chain of 130 therefore gave the same maximum 9 m/s (32.4 km/h) drive bonus as a few strokes. In current EGG3 roads, `RollRhythm.speedBonus` combines the charge with a logarithmically growing chain benefit:

`charge × (9 + 6 × log2(1 + max(0, chain − 4) / 16))` m/s.

The first four strokes retain their earlier charge progression. Every subsequent stroke adds a smaller positive amount; there is no chain ceiling. At full charge, ×130 gives about +100 km/h of target speed. The stroke impulse also grows with the extra bonus, so a long series can overcome the shell's contact losses. The HUD reads the same bonus as the simulation rather than using the old fixed multiplier. The speedometer expands in 80 km/h steps when necessary. Flight freezes the chain/charge, idle rolling discharges them, and retry resets everything. EGG1/EGG2 physics, score coefficients, saved records and map codes remain unchanged.

The speed plunge had another problem: its motor applied −4 m/s² above its target speed, and its very grippy surface dissipated momentum through the asymmetric shell. Earlier 265–273 km/h values were peak simulation speeds, including airborne motion; they did not establish sustained speed on the road. The new speed-course surface uses friction 0.25 with the minimum combination rule, stops applying the motor brake, and damps outward shell hops beside the road with a bounded local normal force. This assistance releases on rescue jumps and actual departures; it cannot grant contact, distance or gates. Ordinary tracks retain their surface and contact behavior. The existing braking climb and falling boundary remain intact.

Comparison uses 150 ms steering corrections with normal input ramping, rather than instant reactions every physics step. With small ±0.65 m corrections, the straight-road run finished with rhythm 140 and a 184.6 km/h peak; the old drive reached about 107 km/h in that comparison. On the revised plunge, two correction amplitudes (0.65 and 1.5 m) both finished and reached about 353–354 km/h **while rolling on the road**, spending 32–40 seconds above 150 km/h. These are deterministic simulation measurements, not a promise of identical human results or an FPS measurement on a Mac.
